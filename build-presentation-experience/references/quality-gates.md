# Quality gates and prohibitions

This is the sole detailed guardrail list. Other references should link here rather than repeat it.

## Product conditions

1. The result is not a stylish PowerPoint converted to the web.
2. Audience understanding changes progressively with the speaker's explanation.
3. One operation has one clear understanding effect.
4. The whole document's logic and landing are represented.
5. The source's brand feels specific rather than generically themed.
6. Every line, color, size, movement, connection, grouping, and convergence has an explainable role.
7. No unsupported fact, number, formula, causal link, category, or conclusion is added.
8. A human judges the running result ready to show people.
9. Decoration does not imply meaning absent from the source or stated presenter intent.
10. Every audience-facing phrase is natural, specific, necessary, and usable in the actual meeting.
11. A selected state is treated as a recommendation unless the source or presenter explicitly
    establishes another meaning; defaults and selection styling must therefore be justified.
12. The experience remains a coherent explanatory document without interaction and becomes more
    understandable—not merely more animated—when operated.
13. Controls look and behave like presentation instruments, not an admin console, data-entry form,
    or generic dashboard.
14. Source qualifiers and precision are preserved: “about,” “estimate,” “forecast,” ranges, and
    significant digits may not be silently strengthened or reformatted into false precision.
15. During questions, the speaker can expose the approved basis, comparison, condition, or detail
    at the relevant moment when the source affords it.

## Ambition conditions

Conditions 1–15 keep the result safe and honest. They do not make it worth remembering. These do:

16. There is a signature moment where the audience sees something the source document alone could
    not show them: a relation, difference, change, or calculated result that is not visible before
    the operation and is understood after it. Visual impact without that change does not pass.
17. The speaker can say in one sentence what the audience should remember, and the experience is
    built to lead into it.
18. A human reviewer would want to keep, forward, or reuse the file — not only view it once.

A fit class of NARRATIVE_ONLY passes review only with the record of every structure considered and
why each was rejected, and a Guided Narrative still changes understanding in stages through
Traverse or Accumulate.

Ambition never licenses a guardrail breach: a memorable fabrication fails condition 7 and is not
delivered. Conditions 16 and 17 are yours to assess in the ambition review.

Only the human may pass conditions 8 and 18. Passing tests never substitutes for them.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- Do not label elements before/after, problem/solution, cause/effect, input/output, old/new, or
  equivalent unless the relation is source-stated, presenter-stated, or human-approved.
- Do not invent a calculation, range, default, total, forecast, ROI, or simulation assumption.
- Do not turn correlation, proximity, chronology, or equal counts into causality or mapping.
- Do not use matching colors to imply a category, size to imply importance, or motion to imply a
  state change without support.
- Do not style a selected option as preferred unless that preference is source- or presenter-backed.
- Do not turn “about 30%” into “30.00%,” an estimate into an actual, or a scenario into a forecast.
- Do not add lines because a composition feels empty or cinematic.
- Do not repair unclear language with smaller type, English, animation, or provenance chrome.
- Do not expose internal chapter names, source IDs, audit terms, or production labels to audiences.
- Do not copy an earlier HTML or rebuild a fixed renderer as a substitute for source-specific work.
- Do not call a wireframe, partial scene, passing build, or positive directional comment a finished
  experience.

## Required audits before final review

- Source coverage: every source page is included, merged, supporting, or excluded with a reason.
- Atomicity: number, unit, and label remain one semantic and visual unit.
- Visual meaning: each semantic expression has source or presenter support.
- Copy: every displayed phrase is approved for an audience.
- Brand: palette, type hierarchy, imagery, and spatial rhythm derive from source evidence.
- Operation: forward, back, reset, keyboard, and touch/scroll behaviors work where provided.
- Presenter utility: approved evidence, comparisons, conditions, or details can be opened during
  questions without leaving the explanation context.
- Access: reduced motion retains the final state and focus remains visible.
- Offline/security: no unexpected external request, console error, or executable injection.
- Responsive: all cues are inspected at desktop and 375px.
- Chrome and fit (look, the verifier cannot see these): on narrow screens nothing scrolls under
  fixed elements such as the brand mark or controls; at 1280×720, 1440×810, and 1920×1080 no scene
  pushes its heading out of view when a new step appears.

Any source-fidelity error, unsupported visual claim, audience-copy rejection, broken core operation,
or unreadable layout blocks delivery. Advisory craft issues may proceed only to human review with
explicit disclosure.
